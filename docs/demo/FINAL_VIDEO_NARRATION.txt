Most AI demos begin with the same question: what can an agent do? Protocol Mirror begins with a harder one. What should an agent never decide?

AI assembles evidence. A human decides. This is Protocol Mirror, a live Web M C P workspace for clinical-trial transparency.

Trial registries show what researchers planned. Publications show what readers eventually see. Protocol Mirror places those records side by side, then gives an agent typed access to stable outcome identifiers and exact source spans.

The agent can inspect the evidence, stage a structured mapping, and bring the relevant human checkpoint into view. Every suggestion cites both sides, labels uncertainty, and explains the mismatch.

But the agent cannot accept or reject its own proposal. That authority exists only here, in the interface, for a human reviewer.

Once the reviewer decides, the capability surface changes from six tools to seven. The new receipt tool exports only reviewed work, exact evidence locators, and the decision trail. Staged suggestions stay out.

Two additional tools retrieve current Clinical Trials dot gov and Pub Med records through bounded, validated adapters. A deterministic fictional case keeps the judging flow reliable, even when a public source is unavailable.

Protocol Mirror makes AI useful precisely where it should never have final authority.
